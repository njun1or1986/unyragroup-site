# Garlic Market Intelligence 2026

Prepared for Unyra Group LLC on 2026-04-15.

## Scope

This research covers:

- fresh/chilled garlic trade (`HS 070320`);
- peeled refrigerated garlic as a convenience/cold-chain subsegment of fresh garlic;
- frozen/IQF garlic as a processed/cold-chain subsegment;
- public-web buyer intelligence by country and product keyword;
- commercial implications for building a 190+ country go-to-market program.

## Executive Summary

The global garlic business is large, liquid, and heavily concentrated. Official 2024 trade data show that fresh/chilled garlic demand is led by Indonesia, the United States, Malaysia, Brazil, and the European Union, while supply is dominated by China by a very wide margin. Public 2025-2026 market intelligence indicates that:

- China remains the anchor supplier to the world market, but buyers are actively de-risking origin concentration and evaluating Egypt, India, Serbia, Uzbekistan, Spain, Argentina and other alternatives.
- The fresh garlic business is still the core global volume driver.
- Peeled refrigerated garlic is a convenience segment with real demand in retail, foodservice, and fresh-cut channels, but it is much more operationally sensitive because temperature abuse creates spoilage and food-safety risk.
- Frozen/IQF garlic is more industrial and food-manufacturing oriented, with growing relevance for puree, diced, roasted, and whole-peeled applications.
- Europe and higher-compliance buyers increasingly reward traceability, pesticide control, microbiological control, consistency, and documented CSR/food-safety systems.

For Unyra, the fastest path to continuous revenue is not to attack 190 countries the same way. The most practical model is:

1. win large-volume fresh garlic markets first;
2. add peeled refrigerated only where cold-chain discipline and shelf-life control are excellent;
3. use frozen/IQF garlic for processors, QSR, industrial kitchens, and ingredient manufacturers;
4. build a multi-origin proposition instead of a single-origin pitch.

## What The Recent Data Says

### 1. Global fresh garlic demand is concentrated in a limited set of very large markets

WITS/World Bank trade data for 2024 place the top fresh/chilled garlic import markets as:

1. Indonesia: USD 747.8M, 555.9M kg
2. United States: USD 313.7M, 125.5M kg
3. Malaysia: USD 313.2M, 232.9M kg
4. Brazil: USD 205.7M, 145.6M kg
5. European Union: USD 192.9M, 84.6M kg
6. Germany: USD 130.6M
7. Netherlands: USD 102.6M
8. United Kingdom: USD 99.6M
9. France: USD 91.3M
10. Italy: USD 88.5M

Commercial meaning:

- Southeast Asia is the center of gravity for very large-volume garlic demand.
- North America remains one of the best value pools.
- Brazil is one of the most relevant volume/value opportunities outside Asia.
- Europe is fragmented but extremely important once quality, residue, and traceability are under control.

### 2. Supply remains highly concentrated in China

WITS/World Bank 2024 exporter data show:

1. China: USD 3.151B, 2.354B kg
2. Spain: USD 475.5M
3. European Union: USD 204.7M
4. Argentina: USD 180.1M
5. Netherlands: USD 144.2M

Implication:

- Any exporter competing internationally must assume China is the reference price-maker in fresh garlic.
- Winning business depends on one of four positions: origin diversification, quality/spec consistency, compliance reliability, or logistics/service speed.

### 3. China shipped record fresh garlic volume in 2025, but at lower average prices

Recent Tridge intelligence published on 2026-02-03 reports that China exported 2.2502 million tons of fresh/chilled garlic in 2025, up slightly from 2024, while average export price fell about 17.4% year over year to about USD 1,078/ton. The same report highlights strong dependence on Indonesia, Vietnam, Malaysia, the Philippines, Bangladesh, the UAE, Thailand, Brazil, Saudi Arabia, and the Netherlands.

Commercial meaning:

- The market is price-competitive.
- Volume growth did not translate into firmer pricing.
- Exporters without cost advantage need differentiation, not commodity positioning.

### 4. Peeled refrigerated garlic is a real convenience segment, but cold-chain discipline is non-negotiable

Current public-web commercial evidence shows:

- Melissa's sells year-round peeled garlic as a refrigerated, ready-to-use product and explicitly states overnight shipping due to perishability.
- Markon lists peeled garlic storage at 34-36 F / 1-2 C and positions it as a ready-to-use product for foodservice.
- FDA published a peeled garlic recall on 2026-04-01 after products were held at insufficient temperatures, creating botulism risk.

Commercial meaning:

- Peeled refrigerated garlic has strong retail and foodservice usability.
- This segment is attractive only if Unyra can guarantee validated cold handling from packhouse to customer.
- It is not enough to sell garlic; the operating model must sell safety and shelf life.

### 5. Frozen/IQF garlic is more industrial and can bypass some fresh-market friction

Recent public-web evidence indicates:

- Frucom markets IQF diced garlic, frozen garlic puree tablets, roasted garlic puree tablets, and frozen whole peeled garlic for food manufacturers and commercial kitchens.
- Tridge price data show frozen garlic export prices from China rising in 2024 versus 2023, roughly from USD 1.08-1.74/kg in 2024 compared with USD 0.90-1.52/kg in 2023.
- On 2026-03-10, the U.S. Commerce notice following court action concluded that Export Packers' IQF cooked garlic cloves from China are outside the scope of the U.S. antidumping order on fresh garlic from China.

Commercial meaning:

- Frozen garlic is not just a backup format; it is a separate commercial lane.
- Frozen/IQF can be especially relevant for ingredient manufacturers, industrial kitchens, ready-meal plants, and distributors needing stable yield and lower prep labor.
- Trade-policy treatment can differ materially from fresh garlic, so regulatory positioning matters.

### 6. Europe rewards de-risked origins and compliance-heavy suppliers

CBI's 2025 Europe studies show:

- European imports of dried garlic from developing origins reached 78,800 tonnes in 2024 and are expected to keep increasing in 2025-2026.
- Buyers still use China heavily, but increasingly look to Egypt, India, Serbia, and Uzbekistan to spread risk.
- Food manufacturers value shelf life and ease of application, while consumers and foodservice still prefer fresh garlic.
- Food safety, traceability, residue control, mycotoxin control, microbiological control, labeling, and consistency are key buying criteria.

Commercial meaning:

- Europe is attractive, but it is not a casual market.
- Unyra must enter Europe with technical sales discipline: COAs, pesticide plan, microbiological plan, traceability, pack specs, and buyer-ready documentation.

## Segment-by-Segment View

## A. Fresh Garlic

Best fit:

- wholesale produce importers;
- terminal market distributors;
- supermarket sourcing arms;
- ethnic food importers;
- garlic repackers;
- foodservice produce distributors.

Winning factors:

- size calibration;
- skin quality and bulb firmness;
- residue compliance;
- stable weekly shipment rhythm;
- origin flexibility;
- clear seasonality story;
- strong logistics execution.

Main risk:

- price wars against Chinese-origin garlic.

## B. Peeled Refrigerated Garlic

Best fit:

- foodservice distributors;
- fresh-cut produce distributors;
- supermarket convenience programs;
- central kitchens;
- meal-kit and ready-to-cook operators.

Winning factors:

- validated refrigerated supply chain;
- shelf-life data;
- pack formats suited to channel;
- microbiological control;
- fast replenishment;
- zero-excuse cold handling.

Main risk:

- cold-chain failure destroys margin and reputation quickly.

## C. Frozen / IQF Garlic

Best fit:

- ingredient manufacturers;
- sauce producers;
- frozen food makers;
- QSR suppliers;
- industrial kitchens;
- large foodservice users.

Winning factors:

- consistent cut size or puree spec;
- Brix/texture/process consistency where applicable;
- industrial pack formats;
- tariff/scope knowledge;
- formulation support for buyers.

Main risk:

- buyers compare on yield, processing behavior, and landed cost, not just raw product quality.

## Commercial Playbook For Uninterrupted Revenue

### 1. Do not sell one SKU; sell three lanes

- lane 1: fresh whole garlic;
- lane 2: peeled refrigerated garlic;
- lane 3: frozen/IQF garlic.

This reduces dependency on one buyer profile.

### 2. Build a multi-origin sourcing story

Needed because buyers increasingly want supply security and risk diversification.

Minimum commercial message:

- primary origin;
- backup origin;
- compliance package by destination;
- pack options;
- lead times;
- MOQ by segment.

### 3. Standardize the sales kit

Unyra should maintain, for each SKU:

- spec sheet;
- photos and pack dimensions;
- cold-chain instructions where applicable;
- phytosanitary and food-safety documents;
- residue/micro testing framework;
- packaging options;
- Incoterms and lead times;
- target customer archetype.

### 4. Separate customer acquisition by channel

- fresh garlic: produce importers and wholesale distributors;
- peeled garlic: foodservice, fresh-cut, retail convenience;
- frozen garlic: industrial ingredient buyers and processors.

### 5. Use country clusters instead of 190 isolated sales efforts

Recommended clusters:

- Southeast Asia
- GCC / Middle East
- Western Europe
- Eastern Europe
- North America
- Andean / South America
- Caribbean
- Africa gateways
- Oceania

## Hard Truths / Limits

- Public-web data is good for global trade ranking by country, but uneven for company-level top-10 buyer ranking across 190 countries.
- Official 2024 WITS reporting for fresh/chilled garlic yields 174 import markets in the accessible table.
- Public buyer directories such as Trademo can extend the commercial target universe beyond the official WITS table, but data cleanliness and completeness vary by market and keyword.
- A fully verified 190-country company-level importer ranking usually requires paid customs intelligence platforms and/or direct customs databases.

So the right interpretation is:

- country-level global ranking: strong and official;
- company-level buyer ranking: strong in priority markets, weaker in long-tail markets unless paid data is added.
