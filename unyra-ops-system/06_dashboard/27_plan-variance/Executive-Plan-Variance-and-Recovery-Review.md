## Variance Severity Tiers

| Severity tier | Condition | Founder posture | Required control action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monitor | Variance is visible but does not yet affect cash, release, customer promise, or founder workload. | Watch without overreacting. | Keep owner, date, and source route current. |
| Correct | Variance affects a quarter priority but has a credible recovery path and no hidden exposure. | Push harder and require proof. | Add dated recovery evidence and KPI movement before next review. |
| Contain | Variance could distort cash, release, capacity, concession, or trust-linked movement. | Stop expansion until proof improves. | Route to capital allocation, assumption control, and source board containment. |
| Escalate | Variance repeats after founder pressure or creates same-day operating risk. | Treat as owner-rhythm or control-system failure. | Route to owner accountability, executable-check control, and founder decision board. |
| Reset | Variance invalidates the commitment or makes the original plan misleading. | Replace the plan language rather than defending it. | Update quarterly plan, forecast, scenario response, and founder decisions. |

## Recovery Evidence Standard

| Recovery proof type | Minimum evidence | Invalid substitute | Founder read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cash recovery | Updated cash forecast, collection status, release posture, and owner action date. | General confidence that payment will arrive. | Cash-sensitive recovery is real only when it changes exposure control. |
| Demand diversification | Pipeline stage movement, qualification proof, next dated customer action, and concentration impact. | A new prospect name without stage discipline. | Diversification is not real until it reduces anchor-account dependence. |
| Supply recovery | Backup-source proof, capacity confirmation, scenario path, and promise-window adjustment. | Verbal supplier comfort or theoretical capacity. | Sourcing recovery must protect committed demand before upside. |
| Live-gate recovery | Current executable-check record, owner-led closeout proof, funding-release posture, and no founder-rescue repeat. | Founder availability at the moment of risk. | Live-gate recovery exists only when the system acts before founder rescue. |
| Pricing recovery | Quote evidence, margin impact, concession reason, recovery route, and founder-decision status. | Commercial urgency or relationship rationale alone. | Pricing recovery must protect realized margin, not just win probability. |

## Escalation and Stop Rules

| Rule | Condition | Required escalation | Founder action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stale recovery stop | Recovery update lacks current source evidence, owner date, or KPI movement. | Route to owner accountability and reject narrative status. | Keep variance open and require proof before next review. |
| Founder-rescue repeat | Same variance or gate requires founder intervention twice in one cycle. | Route to owner accountability, executable-check control, and founder decision board. | Intensify, narrow scope, or reset owner expectation. |
| Capital containment stop | Recovery asks for cash, release, concession, or capacity while proof remains weak. | Route to capital allocation, cash forecast, and relevant source board. | Pause expansion until capital posture is reconciled. |
| Forecast reset trigger | Forecast confidence remains high after validation date slips or assumption proof weakens. | Route to revenue forecast, assumption control, and quarterly plan. | Reset confidence band and update plan language. |
| Scenario activation trigger | Variance threatens supply, payment, release, or counterparty timing. | Route to scenario response and risk register. | Activate fallback path or containment language. |
